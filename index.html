Background

Biography

Paul Biya was born on February 13, 1933, in Mvomeka'a, Cameroon. He became president on November 6, 1982, after serving as prime minister. His presidency has shaped the country's political and public life for decades.

His long tenure is viewed differently by supporters and critics, and remains an important subject in discussions about Cameroon's modern history.

Studies and early career

Early Life and Education

Biya completed his early education in Cameroon and continued his higher education in France. His studies included public law and political science.

  • Degree in public law (Licence en droit public), earned in 1960
  • Studies at the Paris Institute of Political Studies (Sciences Po)
  • Further studies in public administration and overseas affairs

Public service

Political Career

  1. 1968–1975Served as Secretary-General of the Presidency.
  2. 1975–1982Served as Prime Minister under President Ahmadou Ahidjo.
  3. November 1982Assumed the presidency following Ahidjo's resignation.
  4. 1985Became national president of the Cameroon People's Democratic Movement (CPDM/RDPC).
  5. 2008A constitutional amendment removed presidential term limits.

In office

Presidency and Public Debate

Priorities and development

His administration has emphasized national unity, infrastructure, education, and development projects. The related milestones and initiatives are outlined on the accomplishments page.

Challenges and criticism

His presidency has also faced criticism and challenges related to economic opportunity, corruption, security, governance, political reform, and human rights.

Legacy

Supporters credit Biya with maintaining stability and pursuing development. Critics argue that further reforms and greater accountability are needed. His legacy continues to be debated in Cameroon and internationally.
